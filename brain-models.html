How Does the ADHD Brain Work Differently?

Explore how differences in brain development, attention, self-regulation and dopamine-related signalling may affect children with ADHD.

Typical Brain

Typical Brain Model

Our model uses green LEDs across different brain regions to illustrate brain activity and the coordinated functioning of areas involved in attention, thinking, learning and emotional regulation.

ADHD Brain

ADHD Brain Model

Our model highlights selected brain regions using red LEDs and represents some regions as comparatively smaller. It is designed to introduce differences in brain development and the functioning of brain networks involved in attention, impulse control, motivation and emotional regulation.

These are simplified educational models, not actual brain scans. ADHD cannot be identified by brain size or LED patterns. Every child with ADHD is unique.
Key Concepts

What Are We Demonstrating?

Three concepts our models are designed to introduce in a simple, visual way.

Prefrontal Cortex

Helps with attention, planning, organising, decision-making and controlling impulses.

Dopamine & Motivation

Dopamine is involved in motivation, reward, attention and learning. Differences in dopamine signalling may contribute to some ADHD-related difficulties.

Emotion & Self-Regulation

Brain networks involved in managing emotions, responding to situations and regulating behaviour may function differently in people with ADHD.

LED Guide

What Do the LEDs Mean?

Green LEDs — represent comparatively higher dopamine activity in Typical Brain model.
Red LEDs — represent comparatively lower dopamine activity in ADHD Brain model.
Scientific Accuracy

Important to Know

ADHD-related brain differences are complex, vary between individuals, and involve development and brain network functioning.

Our models do not claim that all ADHD brains are smaller or that all ADHD brains have uniformly lower dopamine.

The red LEDs do not accurately measure reduced brain activity — they highlight selected regions to spark educational discussion.

🧬

These models help parents, students and teachers understand that ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition involving differences in brain development and connectivity — not a result of laziness or poor parenting. Early understanding and the right support can make a meaningful difference.
